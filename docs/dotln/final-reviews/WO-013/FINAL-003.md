## Implementation review

The change applies the Design as written, and the convention is legible from the directories. The shell owns presentation through two projection templates. The menu renders one `nav` labelled through the translation pipe and projects its groups. The group renders an `h2` for the projected heading and a `ul` whose `[menu-item]` slot falls back to a translated empty line, so an empty group reads "No tools yet." without a conditional in the shell. Neither template injects anything or imports the router. The app owns navigation: three projected groups, and `routerLink`, `routerLinkActive` and `ariaCurrentWhenActive` on each link, built from `buildRoute`. The browser spec's single `aria-current="page"` on every route therefore follows from the router alone. The layout's `[nav]` slot became a neutral `div.navigation`, which is right once the menu carries the landmark; the layout's spec and story now supply their own `nav`. The arriving pages are the smallest containers that satisfy the Design: a heading, the house empty state with a translated sentence, and a router link back to the board, with no facade and no store. Each feature library exports its routes and nothing else, keeps its imports relative and ships a `testing` entry point with one provider kit. Both libraries reuse the board library's AnalogJS runner and strict compiler configuration, because the installed Nx test generator refused Vitest 5 ([D002](../../evidence/WO-013/decisions.md#wo-013-d002)); the generator log records the refusal and the fallbacks. D003's comparison, projected groups over fixed slots, fits the operator's road: a fourth group costs the app two projected elements and the shell nothing. A simpler form that holds the same contract would put the links in the shell, and the Design rejects that for the router rule. I found none simpler.

What a maintainer may not understand in six months: why the layout's slot is a `div` while the menu is a `nav` (one landmark, owned by the menu; `sub-apps.md` says so); why the shell is `type:feature` while its templates refuse injection and the router (the notices orchestration since WO-007; `sub-apps.md` says so); why the arriving pages name an order in user-facing text (the Design asks for it and the specs assert it); and why `app.scss` gives links `display: inline-block` (the browser spec measures each link's bounds). Three observations, none a defect in the declared behavior. A group given neither an item nor an `emptyMessage` renders an empty list entry; no group in the app does that, and the architecture document tells the next author to pass a message. The feature test kits restate their `en.json` strings, so a spec passes on the kit's copy rather than the shipped file, which is the house pattern the board's kits follow; `i18n:check` guards the keys, not the wording. `sub-apps.md` says to put test providers in `testing/src/public_api.ts`, where the libraries define them in `testing/src/lib/*-test-kits.ts` and re-export them there. Both forms work, and the board does the same.
