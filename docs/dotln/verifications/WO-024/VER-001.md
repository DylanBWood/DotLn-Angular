## What the studies taught me

A level of description is one vocabulary for talking about a thing, set by the question you are asking of it. The test line, the guard's refusal, the gate row, the assignment and the plan are accurate accounts of one afternoon. Each answers its own question: was the edit saved, did the links work, what was promised, why this first. None replaces the others and none is the real one; "ok" is two printed characters at one level and a verdict at the next. Going up a level means asking a broader question and opening a different record, not zooming into the same one, and a higher level can seal off detail a lower one still holds.

A loop is strange when each step moves you to a different level, from marks to meaning or from an object to what it depicts, and the steps still bring you back to where you began. A clock returns to twelve without changing level, so its loop is ordinary. The second card sends your eyes from its ink to its meaning and back to its own letters. In Print Gallery you go from the gallery to the print on its wall, into the town the print depicts, and that town contains the gallery you started in. The return through a change of level is what makes the loop strange.

Collaboration mode should end when the question it was opened for is answered and someone outside the joint sessions can do the routine work alone from what was published. In the study, a Checkout engineer who missed the pairing creates a preview, diagnoses a failed build and removes the preview from the instructions. Release Platform then owns and supports the service, and Checkout owns its builds. It also ends when the agreed time box runs out first, ten working days in the study. The teams then close it anyway, record what failed and decide whether a smaller trial is worth starting. The mode is meant to be short and to hand over to another mode, usually X-as-a-Service.
