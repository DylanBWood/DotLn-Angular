**In my own words, from the studies alone.** Collision avoidance predicts when two bodies' centres will be nearest if neither changes velocity, and how far apart they will be at that moment. It works from their relative position and velocity. If that moment is under 1.5 s away and the predicted gap is smaller than their combined radii, the courier accelerates directly away from the predicted offset. It repeats the forecast every update, so here the warning at 1.25 s lapses by 0.20 s, once the courier's new velocity predicts a gap of 2.42 against a contact distance of 2.00. Flee mirrors Seek by reversing one subtraction: Seek takes target minus body, Flee takes body minus threat, and the same maximum acceleration is applied along the result. The mirror is not exact in this engine, whose Flee fades to nothing at 20 units. Either way it only changes velocity over time, so a body already moving inward keeps coming until it is braked. A firing solution has no answer when, at the fixed launch speed, the discriminant speed⁴ − gravity × (gravity × d² + 2 × h × speed²) is negative, because then no launch angle reaches the target. Here the beacon at 32 m gives 57,600 and two arcs; moved to 48 m it gives −70,400, so the launcher stays silent and no flight is drawn.
