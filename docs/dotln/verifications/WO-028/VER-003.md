**In my own words, from the studies alone.** Collision avoidance predicts when two moving bodies' centres will be nearest if neither changes velocity, and how far apart they will be at that moment. Subtracting one body from the other gives a relative position and velocity, and the time follows from those. If that time is positive and under 1.5 s and the predicted gap is smaller than their combined radii, the courier accelerates directly away from the predicted offset. The forecast is redone at every update, so here the warning at 1.25 s lapses by 0.20 s, once the courier's new velocity predicts a gap of 2.42 against a contact distance of 2.00. Because it trusts current velocities, a turning neighbour can make it wrong, and a dead-on approach leaves no direction to flee along.

Flee mirrors Seek by reversing one subtraction: Seek accelerates along target minus body, and Flee along body minus threat. The mirror is not exact in this engine, whose Flee fades with distance to nothing at 20 units and gives no push at exact coincidence. Either way the push only changes velocity over time, so a body already moving inward keeps coming until it is braked.

A firing solution has no answer when, at the fixed launch speed, the discriminant speed⁴ − gravity × (gravity × d² + 2 × h × speed²) is negative, because then no launch slope reaches the target. Here the beacon at 32 m gives 57,600 and two arcs. Moved to 48 m it gives −70,400, so the launcher stays silent and no flight is drawn. Aiming more precisely cannot help.

**What the operator learns.** Which steering ideas the scene's rail lacks is readable as VER-001 found: Flee, Collision avoidance and Obstacle and wall avoidance are not on the rail, which this repair does not touch, and each study's limits say where it fails.
