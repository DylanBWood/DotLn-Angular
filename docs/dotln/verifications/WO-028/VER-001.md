**In my own words, from the studies alone.** Collision avoidance takes the two bodies' relative position and velocity and works out when their centres will be closest if neither changes velocity, and how far apart they will be then. If that moment is within the 1.5 s window and the projected gap is under their combined radii, the courier accelerates directly away from the projected offset; the forecast is redone every update, so a neighbour that turns can make it wrong. Flee mirrors Seek in direction: Seek's offset is target minus position and Flee's is position minus threat, with the same normalising and acceleration, so the push points the other way. The mirror is not exact here, because this engine's Flee fades to nothing at 20 units where Seek keeps its full pull, and in both cases an acceleration only changes velocity over time, so a body moving inward keeps coming until the push has braked it. A firing solution has no answer when the discriminant speed⁴ − gravity × (gravity × d² + 2 × h × speed²) is negative: at that fixed speed no launch angle reaches the target. For a level shot that means beyond speed² / gravity, 40 m here, so the beacon moved to 48 m gives −70,400 and the launcher withholds the shot.

**What the operator learns.** A study of something on the rail links to the scene (Seek reads Run it in the soldier scene); Flee, Collision avoidance and Obstacle and wall avoidance do not, and each study's limits say where it fails. So the Objective's learning holds without a new page.
